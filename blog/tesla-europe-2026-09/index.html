Tesla in Europe, September 2026: A Two-Speed Market and the Road to the FSD Vote

By Carlo Published September 15, 2026 9 min read

Since our last Europe report in late July, Tesla's European picture has split in two. In August, France and Germany posted triple-digit growth while Norway, Spain, Sweden and Italy fell hard. On the regulatory side, FSD Supervised picked up a sixth EU country and, more importantly, France moved from objecting to testing. Here is what changed between late July and mid-September 2026, what it means if you own or are about to order a Tesla in Europe, and what is still unconfirmed.

August 2026 registrations: a two-speed Europe

National registration data for August arrived in the first days of September. The pattern is not "Tesla up" or "Tesla down" but a clean divide between markets.

Three honest caveats. First, August is a small, holiday-distorted month, so percentages swing hard on modest volumes (Sweden's 123 cars is a drop of 87). Second, the France and Germany gains come off a very weak August 2025, the trough of Tesla's European slump. Third, these are national figures only. ACEA does not publish July separately; its next EU-wide release, covering July and August together, is scheduled for late September, and we will fold it into the October report.

Norway deserves a closer look because it is the market where Tesla is most dominant. The Norwegian road traffic information council OFV noted that without Tesla's decline the Norwegian market would have grown 17% in August, and that Toyota and Volkswagen have narrowed the year-to-date gap to Tesla considerably. Tesla is still Norway's number one brand in 2026, but no longer by the margin it enjoyed.

Why the split: subsidy calendars again

The divide lines up with incentive timing far better than with anything Tesla did in August. In July we wrote that European Tesla numbers follow subsidy calendars; August is the clearest example yet.

  • Germany has a new income-tested purchase grant of up to €6,000 for battery-electric cars, applied retroactively to purchases from January 1, 2026, with a €3 billion budget running to 2029. More than 103,500 applications had been filed by August 5. Germany's record EV month is consistent with that programme pulling buyers in.
  • France hit a record 38.3% battery-electric share in August, and electric cars became the country's most-registered powertrain for the first time. The social leasing scheme reopened on July 16 with 50,000 cars, but it targets low-income households and cheaper models, so it is not the main Tesla driver.
  • Spain only opened its Plan Auto+ application portal on August 4 (up to €4,500 for private buyers, first come first served, retroactive to January 1, open until December 31). Buyers who can wait for certainty tend to wait, which fits a weak August.
Buyer relevance: in 2026 the size and timing of your national incentive matters more to your final price than any quarter-end push from Tesla. Check the programme's budget status and eligibility rules (income, price caps, application deadline) before you configure, not after.

FSD: six countries, French road tests, no vote date

Two things moved since July, and one thing did not.

Slovenia became the sixth EU country to allow FSD Supervised, announced on September 8 by infrastructure minister Jernej Vrtovec. Slovenia did not run its own tests; it recognised the Dutch RDW approval. That puts the list at the Netherlands, Lithuania, Estonia, Denmark, Belgium and Slovenia. By our calculation those six countries hold less than a tenth of the EU's population, which matters because a bloc-wide decision at the Technical Committee on Motor Vehicles (TCMV) needs a qualified majority: at least 15 of 27 member states representing at least 65% of the EU population.

France changed posture. In July, transport minister Philippe Tabarot publicly rejected the Dutch-led route over speed-limit handling and driver-attention warnings. On September 3 Reuters reported that France has started on-road tests with two FSD-equipped cars supplied by Tesla, after what Tabarot called "a constructive exchange" with Elon Musk. France aims to finish its assessment by mid to late September. Because France carries roughly 15% of the EU population, its vote is one of the few that can make or break the 65% threshold on its own terms.

What did not move: the vote date. The widely repeated October 6 date traces back to Tesla itself. A French transport ministry source told Reuters the vote could come "next month or early December". We have not found an official TCMV agenda confirming either. Sweden is also worth reading precisely: minister Andreas Carlson told the Riksdag at the end of August that the government's ambition is EU-wide approval of systems like FSD Supervised, provided safety can be guaranteed, and the Swedish Transport Agency says a broader launch in Sweden would come "at the earliest in spring or summer 2027" if member states vote yes.

What is not settled: Reuters reported on August 6 that Tesla pressed RDW to keep its FSD safety file confidential, and that regulators in six countries declined to release test data. Tesla released a self-produced FSD safety dataset in early September; those are Tesla's figures, not independently verified. Expect transparency to be part of the debate before any vote.

For the approval mechanics, see FSD approval in Europe explained and our country-by-country guide when will FSD come to my country.

Recalls and safety: one OTA fix, one open question

  • EU recall 2026_103555 (Model 3 and Model Y). Software update 2026.27.200 restores compliance with UN Regulation 135, the pole side-impact standard, by changing front side airbag behaviour. Tesla's release notes say no service visit is needed once the update installs. If your car has not taken 2026.27.200 or later, it is worth checking.
  • Door handles: no European recall, for now. After China recalled about 2.98 million Teslas over emergency door-release mechanisms, the Dutch regulator RDW told Reuters on August 26 that there is currently no Tesla recall in Europe. RDW said it is monitoring the issue and declined to say whether it is investigating. Treat this as open.

Product and Giga Berlin

  • Model Y interior change. A new Zen Grey interior replaced the Black and White option on Model Y Premium (RWD and AWD) in European configurators, including Germany, the UK, France and the Nordics, at the same option price as the white interior it replaces.
  • Model Y L: still not orderable in Europe. The long-wheelbase six-seater has EU type approval and code references have appeared in European configurators, but there is no official European price or order date. Any European price you see online is speculation. Our Model Y L Europe guide tracks it.
  • Tesla Semi comes to Europe. Tesla is showing a European-spec Semi at IAA Transportation in Hannover (September 15 to 20), quoting up to 550 km of range at 40 tonnes, with European customer deliveries slated for 2027. No European price yet.
  • Giga Berlin runs extra shifts. Handelsblatt reported on September 4 that Grünheide is producing fewer than 6,500 cars a week and targets 7,500 a week from mid-October, with three mandatory extra shifts on September 18, 19 and 25 agreed with the works council. A plant adding shifts at quarter-end is a supply signal worth more than any headline about demand.

TSLA, SpaceX and Terafab

TSLA closed at $358.97 on September 14, up about 14.7% from $313.03 on July 24, the day after the Q2 earnings sell-off we covered in our Q2 margin analysis. The ride was not smooth: the stock fell 5.9% on September 4 after a low-key Cybercab launch event left pricing and production questions unanswered and the US safety regulator NHTSA opened an audit into how Tesla self-certified the vehicle.

On the Tesla and SpaceX question, the facts moved a little and the speculation moved a lot:

  • Terafab became official on August 6. Tesla and SpaceX jointly announced a $16.8 billion first phase of a chip factory in Grimes County, Texas, with at least 3,000 jobs. How the cost and ownership split between the two companies has not been disclosed, and the Texas governor's announcement credited SpaceX alone.
  • SpaceX's first quarterly report as a public company confirms the commercial link. Its Q2 Form 10-Q, filed August 4, discloses in Note 17 that SpaceX bought $295 million of Megapacks from Tesla in Q2 and $329 million in the first half.
  • No deal filings. As of September 15, EDGAR shows no Tesla 8-K since July 22 and no merger-related filing from either company. Tesla has not yet set a date for its 2026 annual meeting.

The September 2026 buyer checklist

  1. Check your incentive first. Germany's grant is income-tested and retroactive; Spain's Plan Auto+ is first come first served until December 31. Confirm budget status on the official programme site on the day you order.
  2. Quarter-end means speed, not necessarily care. Giga Berlin's extra shifts point to a strong late-September delivery push. Stock cars move fastest now; if you want an unhurried handover, early October may be calmer.
  3. Do not order for an FSD date. Even an October vote in favour would not mean activation everywhere the same week, and Sweden's regulator already talks about 2027. Buy the car that makes sense without FSD. Our FSD vs Enhanced Autopilot guide helps with the choice.
  4. Check software on a used Model 3 or Model Y. Confirm the car has received 2026.27.200 or later so the R135 recall remedy is installed.
  5. Verify referral status before ordering, not after. Tesla referral benefits vary by country, model and date, so verify current terms in the Tesla app or configurator. They cannot be added retroactively. How Tesla referrals work.
  6. Compare neighbouring markets if you have flexibility; our cheapest-country comparison shows the spreads are real, but factor in your own country's incentive rules.

FAQ

How did Tesla sell in Europe in August 2026?

Very unevenly. Registrations rose 279% in France and 111% in Germany, and doubled in Denmark, but fell 79% in Norway and Spain, 41% in Sweden and 36% in Italy. EU-wide ACEA figures for July and August are due in late September 2026.

When will the EU vote on Tesla FSD Supervised?

No date is officially confirmed. Tesla has pointed to a vote as early as October 6, 2026, while a French transport ministry source told Reuters it could come in October or early December. As of September 15, six EU countries allow FSD Supervised: the Netherlands, Lithuania, Estonia, Denmark, Belgium and Slovenia.

Is there a Tesla recall in Europe in September 2026?

Yes, one software recall: EU recall 2026_103555 for Model 3 and Model Y restores compliance with UN Regulation 135 via over-the-air update 2026.27.200, with no service visit needed. On door handles, the Dutch regulator RDW said on August 26 there is currently no recall in Europe, after China recalled about 2.98 million Teslas.
