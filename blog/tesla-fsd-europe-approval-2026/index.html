Tesla FSD Supervised in Europe 2026: Netherlands RDW Approval Explained

Published March 5, 2026 · Updated October 1, 2026 · 9 min read

Status October 1, 2026: As of October 1, 2026, eight European countries allow FSD Supervised: the Netherlands (RDW type approval on April 10, 2026), Lithuania, Estonia, Denmark, Belgium, Slovenia (September 8), Czechia (September 21) and Croatia (September 29, rollout pending). The approved software runs on HW4 (AI4) cars only. An EU-wide decision is not on the agenda for the October 6 TCMV meeting and is not expected before December. This article was first written in March, when approval was still pending; the sections below have been updated where the facts changed.
🆕 March 24 Update — Smart Summon EU Cap Removed: EU Regulation 2026/481 takes effect March 24, 2026, removing the 1,500-vehicle annual cap on Automated Valet Parking (AVP) type approvals. This clears the way for Tesla to seek full type approval for Actually Smart Summon in Europe without restrictions — currently limited to 6 metres range due to UNECE R79 rules. Tesla has not yet announced a timeline for expanded Smart Summon, but the regulatory barrier is now removed.

For years, European Tesla owners watched North American drivers use Full Self-Driving while their own cars were locked to basic Autopilot. That changed in 2026. On April 10, 2026 the Dutch vehicle authority RDW granted the first European type approval for FSD Supervised under UN Regulation 171, after a March target date slipped. By October 1, seven more countries had adopted it.

This is not a vague roadmap promise. Tesla has already conducted a public city demonstration in the Netherlands, navigating real Dutch urban traffic — cyclists, roundabouts, tram crossings and all — with RDW officials watching, before the April approval.

Here is what this means for Tesla buyers across Europe, which countries are next, and what you need to know if you are buying now.

What Is Tesla FSD Supervised?

Tesla Full Self-Driving (FSD) Supervised is the company's most advanced driver assistance system. Unlike standard Autopilot — which handles highway lane-keeping and adaptive cruise control — FSD Supervised can navigate:

  • City streets with traffic lights, stop signs and complex intersections
  • Roundabouts, including multi-lane European roundabouts
  • Dense urban environments with cyclists, pedestrians and narrow lanes
  • Unprotected left turns (right-hand turns for UK drivers) across oncoming traffic
  • Highway lane changes and automatic on/off-ramp navigation

The "Supervised" designation is important: the driver must remain alert and ready to take over at any moment. This is Level 2+ automation, not fully autonomous driving. Tesla's cameras and neural network do the work — but you are legally responsible for the vehicle at all times.

💡 Key distinction: FSD Supervised ≠ self-driving. The driver must watch the road and be ready to intervene. Think of it as an extremely capable co-pilot, not an autonomous chauffeur.

The Netherlands: First EU Approval

The Netherlands' RDW (Rijksdienst voor het Wegverkeer — the Dutch vehicle authority) worked with Tesla on the type-approval process for FSD Supervised through late 2025 and into 2026. After a missed March 20 target, the RDW granted type approval under UN Regulation 171 on April 10, 2026, covering HW4 (AI4) vehicles. Other member states can adopt the Dutch approval nationally; an EU-wide decision needs a qualified majority at the Technical Committee on Motor Vehicles (TCMV).

Why the Netherlands first? Several factors:

  • Regulatory agility: The Netherlands has a track record of moving faster on EV and autonomous vehicle regulation than larger EU markets
  • Tesla's presence: Tesla's European headquarters is in Amsterdam. The RDW and Tesla have a close working relationship
  • EU precedent: A Netherlands approval under EU vehicle approval frameworks means other member states can mutually recognise the approval — accelerating the rollout to Germany, France, Italy and Spain
  • Road complexity: Dutch roads include some of Europe's most challenging urban environments: cyclists at every intersection, narrow streets, trams. If FSD works there, it works anywhere in Europe

The Amsterdam City Demo — What We Saw

In early March 2026, Tesla conducted a public demonstration of FSD Supervised in Dutch city traffic. This was not a closed course or controlled environment — it was real Amsterdam urban driving, with RDW officials in attendance.

The demo covered:

  • Navigating a busy city centre intersection with cyclists approaching from multiple directions
  • Handling a multi-lane roundabout with mixed traffic
  • Making protected and unprotected turns in dense traffic
  • Responding correctly to Dutch road markings and traffic light sequences

This public demo is a significant signal. Tesla does not typically demonstrate FSD publicly during regulatory review — the fact that this demo happened, and was reported (basenor.com, March 4 2026), showed the RDW was engaged with the system's performance. Despite this, the March 20 full UN-R-171 approval target was missed — the decision has been deferred to April 10, 2026 (confirmed by RDW).

Which EU Countries Have Followed the Netherlands?

The summer 2026 EU-wide target did not happen. Instead, countries have adopted the Dutch approval one by one. Status on October 1, 2026:

✅ Allowed (8)

Netherlands, Lithuania, Estonia, Denmark, Belgium, Slovenia, Czechia, Croatia

Netherlands: RDW type approval April 10, 2026. Slovenia: September 8. Czechia: September 21. Croatia: approved September 29, rollout announced as coming soon.

🇫🇷 France

Testing, no decision

Rejected the Dutch route in July over speed-limit handling and driver attention. Started on-road tests with two Tesla-supplied cars in early September (Reuters).

🇩🇪 Germany

Not available

On October 1, 2026 the German configurator still marks FSD Supervised as not yet available, pending regulatory approval.

🇸🇪 Sweden

Earliest spring or summer 2027

The Swedish Transport Agency says a broader launch would come at the earliest in spring or summer 2027, and only if member states vote yes.

🇪🇺 EU-wide vote

Not before December 2026

The October 6 TCMV agenda lists only a continued discussion of the Dutch application, with no vote. A decision needs at least 15 of 27 member states representing at least 65% of the EU population.

🇬🇧 United Kingdom

Separate process

Not covered by EU decisions; the UK runs its own approval process.

Spain and Italy have not announced a decision. For a country-by-country view, see when will FSD come to my country.

Norway, Switzerland, and other non-EU European markets will need their own approval processes — though these countries typically align closely with EU regulations and tend to move quickly.

Is Your Tesla FSD-Ready? Hardware Check

Important: the FSD Supervised build approved in Europe runs only on Hardware 4 (AI4). Here is the quick check:

1
Hardware 4 (HW4) — All Model 3/Y produced since mid-2023, all new Model S/X. The hardware covered by the RDW approval.
2
Hardware 3 (HW3) — Model 3/Y produced 2019–2023, Model S/X 2021+. Not covered by the European approval. Tesla has talked about a reduced version for HW3, and Dutch HW3 owners have started a collective claim. See our HW3 guide.
3
Hardware 2.5 and below — Very early Teslas (pre-2019). These vehicles may not support FSD Supervised without a hardware upgrade. Check your car's MCU version in the vehicle's touchscreen under Software.

To check your hardware version: tap the Tesla logo on your touchscreen → Software → scroll to see your Autopilot computer version. If it says "Full Self-Driving Computer" or "FSD Computer," you are hardware-ready.

How Much Does FSD Cost in Europe?

Tesla FSD pricing in Europe has changed several times. What we saw on October 1, 2026:

  • Monthly subscription: the German configurator offers the FSD package at €99 per month (Norway: NOK 1,090 per month), while marking FSD Supervised itself as not yet available there.
  • One-time purchase: we did not see a one-time purchase option in the German or Norwegian configurator on October 1, 2026. Check your own market, because options differ by country.
  • Included at no extra charge: FSD is not included in any standard Tesla model in Europe. It is always a paid upgrade.
Pricing note: earlier versions of this article quoted one-time prices from early 2026. Tesla has since moved Europe toward a monthly subscription, as in the US. Verify the current option in your configurator before ordering.

One important note: Tesla has changed FSD transfer rules in the US (most recently affecting Cybertruck AWD buyers in early 2026). These US changes do not affect EU buyers — FSD purchased in Europe stays linked to your vehicle's VIN.

Should You Buy a Tesla Now or Wait for FSD?

This is the practical question for most European buyers. Here is the honest answer:

✅ Buy now if:

  • The car makes sense without FSD; new Teslas ship with HW4, which the European approval covers
  • You want to combine an order with an active referral

⏳ Wait if:

  • FSD is your primary reason for buying — you want to see it live first
  • You are in Germany/France/Spain and want FSD available from day one
  • You prefer to watch the Netherlands rollout before committing

Our view: do not order for an FSD date. Buy the car that makes sense without FSD; a new HW4 Tesla can get FSD Supervised over the air once your country allows it. Outside the eight countries above, there is no confirmed date.

What is the difference between FSD Supervised and Autopilot?

Autopilot handles highway driving (lane-keeping, adaptive cruise, lane changes). FSD Supervised adds full city driving capability — traffic lights, stop signs, complex intersections, roundabouts. Both require driver supervision.
